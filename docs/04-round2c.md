## 2. Full stress tests

### 2.1 Group Gift Decks ✅ 75

**Pitch:**
1. An organizer starts a deck for a person or couple: a milestone birthday, retirement, bridal shower or bachelorette, graduation, going-away, Valentine's, or a family holiday game.
2. They share **one link**. Each friend adds cards from a template:
   - **Trivia about the honoree**
   - **"52 reasons we love you"**
   - **"Open when…" notes**
   - **Advice / date-night ideas**
   - **Inside-joke prompts**
3. The app auto-typesets 54 cards plus a printed box.
4. A print-on-demand partner prints and ships it. A print-at-home PDF is the fallback for last-minute buyers.

**T1 — Competitors (5, thin):**

| Competitor | Covers our wedge? | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| [MakeMyCards](https://www.makemycards.art/) | Yes: group link + printed deck, but **Cards-Against-Humanity-style humor**, not gift formats | Thin: indie side project ([itch.io devlog](https://mattmmc.itch.io/makemycards/devlog/1575301/you-can-now-order-a-printed-makemycards-deck)), printed decks added recently, no visible traction |
| [Inside Joke](https://insidejoke.app/) | Party humor, AI-written cards, "reserve your printed copy" | Thin / pre-launch |
| [Bundle](https://www.thebundlegame.com/) | Hand-made custom *board games* from a questionnaire, ~$60 | Small (~$16.5K in its first 4 months, 2018 ([Side Hustle School](https://sidehustleschool.com/episode/620/))); human-made, slow |
| Etsy sellers | Hand-made custom trivia and "52 reasons" decks ([example: 76 reviews](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1038758046/personalized-trivia-game-custom-trivia)) | Proves demand; manual, single-author |
| [MakePlayingCards](https://www.makeplayingcards.com/design/52-reasons-why-i-love-you-cards.html) templates | Single-author "52 reasons" decks | DIY design |
| GroupTogether, Thankbox, Kudoboard | Group *messages* → card or book | A different format; would need a separate printer |

**T2 — Substitute (5).** Google Form + Canva card template + upload to MakePlayingCards: **2–4 hours** of collecting, copying and layout, not 30 minutes. ChatGPT can't collect from 15 friends or ship a physical deck.

**T3 — Demand (6).**
- Etsy has many "52 reasons" and "who knows the bride best" listings with bestseller badges and reviews ([52 reasons](https://www.etsy.com/market/52_reasons_i_love_you_deck_of_cards), [who knows bride best](https://www.etsy.com/market/who_knows_bride_best)). Amazon and Zazzle sell generic versions.
- Kudoboard proves people pay for group-made keepsakes.
- Occasions are huge (about 2M weddings a year, plus showers and bachelorettes, milestone birthdays, retirements, grads) ([Knot](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-knot-worldwide-unveils-2026-real-weddings-study)).
- Keywords (est., low confidence): "52 reasons why I love you" ~20–40K/mo (peaks in February), "who knows the bride best" ~10–20K/mo, "custom trivia game" ~2–5K/mo.

**T4 — Reach (6).**
- **Built-in invites:** each deck exposes 5–30 contributors, and many will organize their own for a later occasion.
- **An Etsy storefront** is a real no-ad marketplace channel. Etsy allows print-on-demand if we disclose the production partner ([Etsy POD rules](https://www.listadum.com/blog/understanding-etsys-rules-for-print-on-demand-sellers)).
- Pinterest ("52 reasons" is a Pinterest staple), TikTok gift-reveal videos, r/weddingplanning, r/bachelorette.
- **When the need hits:** 1–4 weeks before the event, the organizer searches for "personalized bridal shower game" or a "gift for mom's 60th".

**T5 — Economics (5).**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Price | **$39 printed deck** incl. US shipping; $15 print-at-home PDF; extra copies $22 |
| COGS | Print: The Game Crafter ~$9.99/deck ([TGC](https://help.thegamecrafter.com/article/48-custom-playing-cards)) plus a printed box (est. +$2–4); shipping est. $5–8; Stripe ~$1.40 → **~$18–23** |
| Gross profit | ~$16–21 per printed deck direct; ~$11–16 via Etsy (after ~10–20% fees) |
| $3K/mo revenue | ~77 decks/mo (≈ $1.4K gross profit) |
| $10K/mo revenue | ~256 decks/mo (≈ $4.6K gross profit) |
| Marginal cost | Print is passed through; optional AI prompt suggestions ≈ $0.01 |
| 90-day fit | Launch ~mid-Nov → Christmas print cutoff is tight (TGC historically advised **ordering by ~Nov 15**; Nov 25, 2025 orders shipped mid-December ([TGC news](https://news.thegamecrafter.com/post/801208361374957568/holiday-orders), [BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1249482/holiday-shipping-deadlines))). **Valentine's Day ("52 reasons") falls inside the window**, and showers and birthdays run all year. $3K/mo in 90 days is plausible; $10K needs Valentine's + Etsy momentum |

**T6 — Pay trigger (8).** Creating the deck is free. Once about 20 cards are in, the organizer sees **a live preview of the real printed deck and box, with friends' names on the cards**, plus the prompt **"Lock the deck and ship by [date] — $39"**. The pay moment lands at peak emotion.

**T7 — Pre-mortem (5):**
1. **Contributors don't show up.** The deck stays half-empty and never gets bought. Mitigation: a 20-card minimum, AI prompt ideas, organizer nudges, template filler cards.
2. **Print and holiday delays** → refunds and bad reviews.
3. **Etsy and Pinterest traction takes months**, and MakeMyCards or Inside Joke add gift templates.

**T8 — Risk (7).** Low liability. Private groups mean little moderation, but we need a report button and to block hateful content on printed items. We must avoid trademarked game names and card text (Cards Against Humanity's license is non-commercial). Print-quality disputes are the main operational risk.

**T9 — Build (7).** 2–4 weeks.
- **Hardest piece:** auto-typesetting that never overflows (text fitting, bleed and safe zones, a proof preview) and integrating the **Game Crafter API**, which supports bulk-creating card faces programmatically ([TGC API Deck](https://www.thegamecrafter.com/developer/Deck.html), [Card](https://www.thegamecrafter.com/developer/Card.html)).
- **Printer notes:** Printful only customizes card *backs* ([Printful](https://www.printful.com/custom-playing-cards)); Printify's face customization is unverified; MakePlayingCards supports full custom faces but I found no public API.

**T10 — Moat (5).**
- Gift-first template library (tested prompts per occasion), print pipeline and quality, Etsy review count, and brand.
- Contributors turning into organizers gives mild network effects.
- Clonable in principle, but no strong incumbent is set up for gifts.

**Weighted:** 5 + 10 + 6 + 12 + 10 + 8 + 5 + 7 + 7 + 5 = **75 → SURVIVE.**

**Wedge vs. the nearest competitor (MakeMyCards):**
1. **Gift formats, not party humor:** trivia about the honoree, 52 reasons, open-when, advice.
2. An **organizer dashboard** with contributor nudges and a "deck locks on [date]" deadline.
3. **Gift-grade output:** a printed box, a dedication card, the organizer's photo on the back.
4. **An Etsy storefront plus Pinterest SEO** as channels MakeMyCards doesn't use.

**Wedge vs. Etsy hand-makers:** instant, collected from the whole group, cheaper, and scalable.

### 2.2 School-Choice Lottery Strategist ⚠️ 67

**Pitch:** for families in cities with centralized school lotteries (start with **SF + DC + NYC kindergarten/middle school**, then Boston, LAUSD magnets, Denver, Chicago):
1. Enter address, grade and priorities.
2. See **odds per school**, simulated from the district's published seats/requests/waitlist data plus crowdsourced outcomes.
3. Get a **list-risk score** ("23% chance of no acceptable match → here's a list with 91%"), deadline and tour calendar, and a waitlist-movement tracker after results.

**T1 (5):** NYC-SIFT is strong but covers only NYC high school. Elsewhere: official data tools, [SF Parent Coalition sheet](https://sfparents.org/sfusd-enrollment/), [GoPublicSF cheat sheet](https://gopublicsf.substack.com/p/sfusd-lottery-cheat-sheet), consultants ([Vicky Keston](https://www.vickykeston.com/), [NYC School Help](https://www.nycschoolhelp.com/high-school); School Search NYC's late-application package is $8,500 ([SSN](https://schoolsearchnyc.com/services/individual-consultations/))).

**T2 (4):** most districts use **deferred-acceptance** matching, where ranking honestly is the best strategy. So the "strategy" value is mostly *odds data*, and much of that is published free (DC waitlist history, SFUSD requests per seat, NYC chance icons). A savvy parent can do this in an evening.

**T3 (6):** strong pain.
- SF Standard: "Gaming the system" ([Mar 2026](https://sfstandard.com/2026/03/16/sfusd-lottery-school-assignment-system/)), a family that got its 8th choice, families leaving SF over the lottery ([Aug 2026](https://sfstandard.com/pacific-standard-time/2026/08/21/stay-sf-go-families-deciding-factor-school-lottery/)).
- The Boston strategy post got 640↑ `[O]`.
- Tens of thousands of applicants per city each year `[K]`.

**T4 (5):** city parent Facebook groups and listservs (often promotion-restricted), city subreddits, per-school SEO. Every city is its own launch.

**T5 (5):** $49 per child per season, or $79 including waitlist tracking to August → ~61 families/mo for $3K, ~204 for $10K. **Very seasonal (Oct–Mar), but our 90-day window is exactly in season:** NYC kindergarten applications open **Dec 9, 2026**, the NYC middle-school deadline is **Dec 11, 2026** ([NYC DOE](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/enrollment/enroll-grade-by-grade/middle-school)), and SF's main round runs until **Jan 30** ([SFUSD](https://www.sfusd.edu/schools/enroll/student-assignment-policy/annual-assignment-highlights)). Marginal cost ≈ $0.

**T6 (7):** the list-risk screen is a strong session-1 result.

**T7 (4):** (1) messy per-city data produces odds nobody trusts; (2) free sheets are "good enough"; (3) one season per year means slow learning.

**T8 (5):** bad odds → a bad outcome → angry parents. Needs clear confidence bands. Low data sensitivity.

**T9 (5):** 3–5 weeks for 2 cities. **Hardest piece:** modeling each district's priority rules each year, plus a Monte Carlo simulation of the deferred-acceptance match.

**T10 (7):** versioned rules per district + historical data + crowdsourced results and waitlist movement compound nicely.

**Weighted:** 5 + 8 + 6 + 10 + 10 + 7 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 7 = **67 → CONDITIONAL.**

## 5. What must be true for School-Choice to earn slot 3 (testable in about 2 weeks, in season)

1. **Odds we can defend:** for at least 2 cities (SF + DC), district data plus a simple simulation reproduce last year's published match rates within ±10 points. That's a desk exercise with public data.
2. **Willingness to pay despite free sheets:** a landing page ("See your real odds + list-risk score, $49") posted in the SFUSD Lottery Support Facebook group, DC Urban Moms and NYC kindergarten parent groups converts **≥ 3% to pre-order** before the Dec 9 (NYC K) / Jan 30 (SF) deadlines.
3. **Moderator approval:** at least 2 of those groups allow the post (or a parent admin shares it).
4. **Honest upside:** even if it passes, it's a seasonal ~$3–6K/mo product unless it expands to 5+ cities, so the city-by-city data work must be sustainable for a solo founder.

If it fails, I'd rather ship with **2 strong finalists** than fill slot 3. A single extra Round 2d search is possible, but the pattern across four rounds is clear: most B2C niches are already covered in 2026.
